Mozart was a child prodigy. From the age of six he toured Europe with his father Leopold and his sister Nannerl, playing for courts from Munich to London. In 1781 he settled in Vienna as a freelance musician, and many of his piano concertos were written for his own concerts there, with Mozart as composer, soloist and director at once.

His keyboard was the **fortepiano**: lighter and quieter than a modern grand, with a quick, clear decay. In a 1777 letter to his father he praised the instruments of the Augsburg builder Johann Andreas Stein for their even tone and reliable damping, and in Vienna he owned a fortepiano by Anton Walter.

The **Sonata in C major, K. 545** is the piece many pianists meet first. Mozart entered it in his own catalogue in 1788 as a sonata "for beginners". Its opening, played on this page, is the Classical style in miniature: a clear melody, an Alberti bass, and perfect proportions. The simplicity is deceptive; playing it *evenly* is hard.

Mozart died in Vienna in 1791, aged 35, leaving 27 numbered piano concertos and a body of solo music that is still central to every pianist's training.
