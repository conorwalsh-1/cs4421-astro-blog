Ravel was born in the Basque town of Ciboure in 1875 and grew up in Paris, where he studied at the Conservatoire, including composition with Gabriel Fauré. His repeated failure to win the Prix de Rome became a public scandal in 1905, when he was eliminated before the final round, and the Conservatoire's director resigned.

He is often grouped with Debussy as an "Impressionist", a label he disliked. His piano writing is famous for its precision. **Jeux d'eau** (1901) opened a new world of shimmering, water-like textures. **Gaspard de la nuit** (1908), after poems by Aloysius Bertrand, ends with *Scarbo*, which Ravel reportedly meant to be more difficult than Balakirev's *Islamey*.

Each movement of **Le Tombeau de Couperin** is dedicated to the memory of friends killed in the First World War. The **Concerto for the Left Hand** was written for Paul Wittgenstein, a pianist who lost his right arm in that war.

Ravel died in Paris in 1937.
