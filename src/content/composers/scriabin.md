Scriabin studied at the Moscow Conservatory alongside Rachmaninoff and began as a composer of Chopin-like preludes, mazurkas and études. Over twenty-five years his music changed almost beyond recognition, from late-Romantic lyricism to harmonies that left traditional keys behind.

He linked musical keys with colours: C red, D yellow, F♯ a brilliant blue. In **Prometheus: The Poem of Fire** (1910) he wrote a part for a *clavier à lumières*, a keyboard of coloured light. The **Scriabin** theme on this site uses his colour scheme, and the journal explores it in depth.

His late works are built from synthetic chords such as the **Mystic chord**, which you can play on this page. He died in Moscow in 1915, leaving only sketches for his planned *Mysterium*, a multimedia work meant to transform its audience.
