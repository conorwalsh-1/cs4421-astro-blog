Marc-André Hamelin, born in Montreal in 1961, is one of the most admired virtuoso pianists of our time. He is known above all for bringing rarely heard, ferociously difficult music back into the concert hall: works by Charles-Valentin Alkan, Leopold Godowsky, Nikolai Medtner and many others, recorded across a long series of albums for the Hyperion label.

His repertoire is not only about difficulty. Alongside the transcendental showpieces he has recorded Haydn's sonatas with the same clarity and wit he brings to Alkan, and critics often single out how *effortless* his most demanding performances sound.

Hamelin is also a composer. His **12 Études in all the minor keys**, published in 2010, continue the tradition of composer-pianists from Chopin and Liszt to Godowsky, often in the form of homages to earlier music.

He brings this site's story into the present: the line that runs from Bach's preludes through Liszt's études is still alive.
