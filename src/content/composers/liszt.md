Liszt was a child prodigy who studied in Vienna with Carl Czerny. In 1832 he heard the violinist Niccolò Paganini in Paris and set out to bring the same daring virtuosity to the piano. Over the next fifteen years he toured Europe as the most celebrated pianist of the age.

He helped invent the modern **solo recital**. Concerts of the time usually mixed many performers; Liszt often played the whole programme himself, and advertisements for his London concerts in 1840 used the word "recitals".

In 1847 he stopped touring as a virtuoso and turned to composing, conducting at Weimar, and teaching, famously without charging fees. He championed other composers too. His piano transcriptions brought Beethoven's symphonies and Schubert's songs into ordinary homes.

His late music is spare and experimental. Pieces such as *Nuages gris* (1881) drift far from conventional harmony, anticipating the twentieth century. Liszt died in Bayreuth in 1886.
