Chopin grew up in Warsaw and left Poland in 1830, just before the November Uprising. He settled in Paris in 1831 and never returned home. Every work he wrote includes the piano, and the great majority are for piano alone.

He preferred the intimate setting of the Paris salon to large concert halls and gave relatively few public concerts. He earned his living largely by teaching and publishing. His favourite instruments were made by **Pleyel**. According to Liszt, Chopin said he played an Érard when he felt unwell, but needed a Pleyel when he felt strong enough to find his own sound.

Chopin turned small forms (the nocturne, the prelude, the mazurka, the étude) into some of the deepest music written for the instrument. His **Études** are technical studies that are also concert pieces. The **24 Preludes, Op. 28**, one in each key, were completed during the winter of 1838–39 in Majorca, where he stayed with the novelist George Sand.

Chopin died in Paris in 1849, aged 39, after years of illness.
