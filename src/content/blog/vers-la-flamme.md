*Vers la flamme* ("Toward the flame"), written in 1914, is one of the last pieces Scriabin finished. It has one idea: start in near-darkness and get brighter and brighter until the piano is blazing. It is only a few minutes long, and the hardest part is pacing.

## What makes it hard

- **Pacing the crescendo.** The whole piece is one long crescendo. Peak too early and there's nowhere left to go.
- **Tremolos.** The second half is built on fast repeated-note and chord tremolos that have to *shimmer*, not hammer.
- **Reading the harmony.** Like the other late works it is built from synthetic chords. Learning to see them as shapes on the keyboard, not individual notes, saves a lot of time.

## My practice plan

1. **Map the chords.** Block every harmony as a solid chord and play the piece as a chorale.
2. **Tremolo from the forearm.** Small, loose rotation. Practise the tremolos slowly with accents on alternate beats, then remove the accents.
3. **Mark three dynamic waypoints**, roughly *pp* at the start, *mf* at the halfway point and *fff* only at the very end, and refuse to go past them early.
4. **Record and listen.** On a recording you can hear whether the "light" grows steadily or jumps.
